# 1. Accounting Fundamentals

## Why Accounting Matters First #! 
Accounting is the "language of business"—it records, reports, and interprets financial transactions so you can make informed decisions about running or investing in a company. It helps track profitability, cash flow, solvency, tax compliance, performance evaluation, and strategic planning. Every serious entrepreneur should understand accounting basics before launching their first venture (Indeed).

## Core Concepts #! 
### The Accounting Equation #!
```assets = liabilities + equity```. A fundamental tool in double-entry bookkeeping: every transaction affects at least two accounts to keep this equation balanced forever. You must know how each type of account changes with debits and credits before diving deeper into financial statements (Eduta).

## Double-Entry Bookkeeping #!
### How It Works #!  
Every business has a ledger listing all transactions categorized by date: what happened, which accounts are affected, who was involved. This system ensures that for every dollar spent somewhere in the company's operations—a debit elsewhere reflects it through credits (edX).

**Rules of Debits and Credits:**
- Increases assets? Debit ↑ | Decrease? Credit ↓  
- Increase liabilities/equity? Debit ↓ | Cred it ↑  

### Real Examples #!  
1. Receive $70 cash from sales → credit revenue account by debiting Cash, or receive inventory on account: debit Inventory (asset), credit Accounts Payable (liability).

## Financial Statements - The Big 3 #!
All businesses produce these three essential reports that communicate their financial position to owners/investors/accountants. They're the primary output of accounting systems and critical for decision-making across all business functions (Coursera skills list):

### Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position) #!  
**What it shows:** A snapshot at a specific moment capturing everything an organization owns, owes, plus shareholders' equity—the net value remaining if they liquidated assets after paying debt.  

Components: Current Assets ($25B cash), Non-current/Long-term; Accounts Payable (what's due in 30 days like $1M); Long-Term Debt obligations with maturity beyond one year (Corporate Finance course topic).

### Income Statement #!" 
Also called the **Profit & Loss** report. It calculates revenues minus expenses over a stated period to show net profit or loss after taxes—essentially how much wealth was generated during that timeframe through operations like sales growth strategies, cost management tactics employed by successful companies including supply chain innovations (operations research basics).

### Statement of Cash Flows #!
Often overlooked yet indispensable because **profit ≠ cash**—you can be profitable on paper but have no liquid funds to survive. Divides inflows/outflows into: operational cash flows from sales/purchases; investing activities for capex like machinery purchases and R&D spending; financing sources including loans raised or equity issuance versus dividend payouts/redeemings made back toward shareholders (Financial Statement Analysis skill area).

### Other Key Reports #!  
- **Trial Balance**: Pre-statement check ensuring debits equal credits before preparing official reports, often called adjusting entries when needed to correct year-end errors discovered late in fiscal periods requiring restatements of published figures released quarterly or annually per regulatory requirements like GAAP/IFRS standards governing accounting globally including US SOX compliance rules enforced by SEC regulators over public companies reporting to investors monitoring them closely.
